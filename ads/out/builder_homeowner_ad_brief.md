# Builder ad brief: house extensions for homeowners

This ad is made for a **builder client** to run under their own Facebook/Instagram Page, aimed at **homeowners**. Video: `builder_homeowner_extension_ad.mp4` (25s, 9:16, music + SFX, readable on mute). CTA: **fill out the form below**, so run it as a Meta **Lead form** campaign.

## Ideal customer (avatar)

**"Sarah and David", 34–45, own a 3-bed semi-d in a commuter town.**
- Two or three kids. The kitchen is too small, there's nowhere to work from home, and the kids are sharing a room they've outgrown.
- They've looked at moving, but they can't find anything better in their area at a price that works, and moving means stamp duty, solicitor and estate agent fees, and leaving the school and neighbours they like. So they're thinking "extend instead".
- They have equity or savings and are ready to spend a serious amount (extensions in Ireland commonly run about €1,800–€2,500 per m² in 2026, roughly €75k–€115k for a 30 m² rear extension).
- They scroll Facebook and Instagram in the evening, after the kids are in bed. That's when this ad finds them.
- People aged 25–44 are the most likely to be planning a big home spend (Credit Union survey, March 2026: 45% of Irish consumers plan a substantial home improvement within two years).

## Pain points (what stops them)

1. **"Builders don't call back."** Not turning up and not answering are among the most common complaints about builders (Which? survey). In a 2014 YouGov survey, 58% of UK homeowners said they find it hard to find a tradesperson they trust, and a quarter of those delay the work because of it.
2. **"The price keeps growing."** In a 2026 survey of 300 UK homeowners, about two thirds went over budget. They're scared of a quote that turns into a much bigger bill.
3. **"It'll drag on for months."** The same survey found 60% ran behind schedule. Living in a building site with kids is their nightmare.
4. **Not knowing where to start:** planning permission, what's allowed, what it'll really cost. Ireland's exempted-development rules for extensions changed in July 2026, so even people who looked into it before aren't sure now. Leave the detail to the site visit, not the ad.

## Offer and hook

- **Hook:** "Outgrown your house… but can't face moving?" It calls out the exact situation in the first second.
- **Offer:** **FREE site visit and written quote** for your extension: no obligation, itemised, in writing, now booking spring 2027.
- **Why it works:** it answers the pains directly (someone actually comes out, and the price is written down and itemised), and "spring 2027" gives a reason to act now (people plan winter, build spring).

> ⚠️ **Confirm with the builder before running:** that they'll do free site visits, give an itemised written quote, and are taking spring 2027 bookings, and that they'll answer the phone. The ad promises all four.

## Lead form (Instant Form)

Keep it short. Three questions plus contact details:
1. **What are you planning?** Kitchen extension / Rear extension / Side extension / Attic or other
2. **Where's the house?** (town or Eircode)
3. **When would you like to start?** Spring 2027 / Summer 2027 / Just planning for now
4. Name, phone, email (pre-filled by Meta)

Set the form to **Higher intent** (adds a review screen) so they confirm their details. That means fewer but better leads.

## Targeting suggestion

- Location: within 25–40 km of the builder's base
- Age 30–55. Leave interests broad; the hook does the targeting.
- Budget: €15–25 a day for the first week, then judge on cost per lead and lead quality (did they answer the phone and want a visit?)
